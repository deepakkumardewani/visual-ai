## Summary

- Matching works. In every lit frame (1440 1-6, 1920 1-3, nowebgl 1-3, /v2) exactly one tile is at full brightness with a rim, and it matches the text: pirate, cave, garden, alien planet, hourglass. A first-time viewer reads the rimmed tile as "this one". The large hourglass stays the biggest object even when dimmed, but the rim and the brightness gap still make the match clear.
- The 0.7 dim fits the quiet brief: the wall recedes without going dark, and the copy column stays the focal point. Do not dim harder.
- The prompt box is the right size. The 160-190 character prompts set as three lines at 1440 and 1920 sit well with the label and padding. The two-line alien prompt (prompt-5) leaves one empty line at the bottom, which is the cost of no layout shift and reads fine. Keep the text top-aligned. At 390 the fixed prompt sets as four lines and the box fits.
- GL and DOM walls match in geometry, lift and dim, but they differ in rim colour and lit-tile fill. That difference is the BLOCKING item and the wash advisory.
- Evidence: the rim colours (pixel-sampled), the GL/DOM mismatch, the wash and the corner overlap at 1920 GL and 1440 DOM are confirmed in screenshots. The additive-blend cause, the hard-coded light colour and the layer-stacking cause are inferred from source.
- Keep: the DOM rim (1.5px copper inset plus a soft inner glow), the 0.7 dim, the fixed-height prompt box with no shift, the spotlight switching off once scroll is above 0 (p5-v3-1440-scrolled-150-paused.png), the reduced-motion rest state with no rim or dim, and the plain static prompt on mobile.
